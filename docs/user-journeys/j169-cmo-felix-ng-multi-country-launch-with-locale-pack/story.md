## §2 — Monday June 1, 15:00–17:42 SGT — content localization QA

After lunch (Felix has a chicken-rice from Maxwell Food Centre, eaten at his desk), he opens the `intelligence` µservice's **content-localization QA dashboard**. The dashboard URL: `https://intelligence.veritem-health-asia-pte-ltd-sg.oyatie.cloud/localization/asean-6-2026-06-15/qa`.

The full localization scope: **7 languages × 4 content surfaces (onboarding wizard + 3 nudge sequences) × ~600 strings = ~16,800 localized strings**. Each string carries:

1. The English source
2. The NLLB-200 AI translation
3. The cultural-adaptation overlay (per country)
4. The human-editor review (1 reviewer per language; 7 reviewers total)
5. The AI-content-transparency attestation (EU-AI-Act-Art-50)

Felix uses the dashboard's stratified-sampling mode. He samples 24 strings at random per language (168 strings total) and reviews each one. He looks for:

- **Linguistic naturalness** — does it read like native copy or like translated copy?
- **Cultural appropriateness** — any unintentional offensiveness or tone-deafness?
- **Health-information accuracy** — translation preserves clinical meaning?
- **Brand-voice consistency** — Veritem's brand voice (warm + practical + non-judgemental) preserved?

For Bahasa Indonesia, one sample: English "Track your blood sugar after each meal — small habits make big differences." → NLLB-200 output: "Lacak gula darah Anda setelah setiap makan — kebiasaan kecil membuat perbedaan besar." → human-editor revision: "Catat gula darahmu setelah setiap makan — kebiasaan kecil bisa membawa perubahan besar." (the editor changed "Lacak" → "Catat" because "Catat" is more natural Indonesian for "record/note" in a health context; "Anda" → "kamu" because Veritem's brand voice for B2C is conversational-friendly not formal; "membuat perbedaan" → "membawa perubahan" because "perubahan" reads more native than "perbedaan" in this context). The cultural-adaptation overlay adds nothing here (no culture-specific dimension). The AI-content-transparency attestation marks it `ai_translated_then_human_edited`.

For Vietnamese, one sample: "Reduce your sodium intake by limiting processed foods" → NLLB-200: "Giảm lượng natri bằng cách hạn chế thực phẩm chế biến sẵn." → human editor: "Hãy giảm muối bằng cách hạn chế thực phẩm chế biến sẵn." (editor changed "natri" → "muối" — sodium → salt — because in conversational Vietnamese health content "muối" reads more native than "natri"; medical Vietnamese uses both but conversational uses salt). Cultural overlay adds nothing. Attestation: `ai_translated_then_human_edited`.

For Thai, one sample: "Walking 10,000 steps a day can help manage your blood sugar" → NLLB-200: "การเดิน 10,000 ก้าวต่อวันสามารถช่วยควบคุมระดับน้ำตาลในเลือดได้" → human editor: same (no revision; the AI got it right). Attestation: `ai_translated_human_reviewed_no_edit`.

Felix completes the 168-string sample at 17:18 SGT. The aggregate QA pass-rate (sampled): 96.4% (162 of 168 strings passed without further revision; the 6 that needed editing have been editing already).
