# j147 — The 200-laid-off cohort forms a private mutual-aid channel

## Cold-open

Detroit, 15:42 ET, Wednesday 2026-05-28. The day after j142. Chris is on his couch. He just opened the alumni-channel opt-in mail from Karim. It said: "We've created a private cohort channel in Community for the 200 people from this RIF. Cryptographically verified former-employer. Tap to join."

He taps.

## Chapter 1 — Joining the cohort (T+0 to T+1h)

### 1.1 The cryptographic verification

When Chris taps Join, his personal-tenant Community client emits a join-request to the alumni sub-tenant Karim provisioned (`<former-employer-tenant>.alumni`). The request carries:
- Chris's personal-tenant principal
- His passkey credential_id
- A cryptographic attestation from the former-employer's identity service: "chris.volkov@<former-employer-tenant> was employed 2024-08-14 → 2026-05-27; separation cause = RIF (eligible for alumni-cohort)"

The attestation is generated by the former-employer's identity µservice using Karim's HR-admin signing key (per ADR-0299 attestation primitive — same primitive used in j143 for DLP attestation, just different content). The signature is verifiable by anyone holding the public key.

The alumni sub-tenant's Cedar policy: `b2alumni.community.cohort.join` PERMIT if:
- Attestation signature verifies against registered HR-admin key.
- Attestation says separation-cause ∈ {RIF, voluntary-good-standing}.
- Member is not under any active legal hold (e.g., trade-secret litigation; none here).

Chris's attestation passes. He joins. Audit emits `CohortMembershipGranted{cohort=RIF-2026-05, member=chris.volkov@<chris-personal-tenant>}`.

### 1.2 What he sees on join

The cohort Community channel loads. He sees:
- The cohort name: "RIF-2026-05 — Mutual Aid Cohort (199/200 joined as of 15:43)."
- 47 active threads.
- A pinned post by Karim (yes, Karim from HR — he stays adjacent to the cohort as a "transparency contact" — read-only; he cannot moderate; cohort members elected 3 of their own as moderators).
- A pinned "house rules" doc: no doxxing, no naming specific managers in critique, no scams, no MLM recruiting, mutual-aid first.
- Moderator list: Tasha (elected lead), Reza (info-broker), Wanda (community-care).

He scrolls. The first thread he reads is "Job leads from people we know — please contribute" by Reza. 47 comments. Each comment is a job lead with: company, role, recruiter contact, source (Reza's network, ex-colleague at company, etc.).

He bookmarks four leads relevant to his role.

### 1.3 The verified-former-employer badge

Chris's Community LinkedIn-mode profile gets a new badge: "Verified Former Employee — <former-employer-tenant>, 2024-08-14 to 2026-05-27." This badge is cryptographically verifiable. When he applies to KrampusCorp (j145), KrampusCorp's recruiters can verify the badge without contacting his former employer (FCRA-compliant; no out-of-band verification call needed).

## Chapter 2 — Daily life in the cohort (T+2d to T+30d)

### 2.1 The morning routine

Every morning Chris checks the cohort channel. Patterns emerge:

- **#job-leads** — a steady drip of leads from network connections.
- **#commiseration** — venting + emotional support; clearly bounded ("don't dwell here longer than feels good").
- **#resources** — links to good resume reviewers, severance-negotiation Twitter threads, COBRA-question explainers.
- **#cohort-discount-referrals** — *this one is interesting*. Tasha works at a network-connection's company that gives "10% off for former-employer cohort members" on their SaaS product. She posts the discount code. 4 cohort members redeem. The benefit ecosystem keeps building.
- **#hiring-tenant-spotlights** — when one cohort member gets an offer (like Wanda at Datadog), they post the news; cohort celebrates; sometimes referrals come back ("we have 3 more openings — I'd recommend you for this one"). This is the cross-tenant referral flywheel.

### 2.2 An anti-scam moment

On day 5, a private-DM scammer joins the cohort. (Community moderation lane.) The scammer pretends to be a "career coach" offering $1,500 packages. Wanda (moderator) flags. Detection-substrate evaluates: pricing-pressure + off-tenant URL + no verified-coach attestation → high HRRP-class-fraud score. Community auto-quarantines the scammer's posts pending moderator review. Tasha + Wanda + Reza agree: kick. The scammer is removed; audit emits `CohortMemberRemovedForFraud`. This is j32's moderation lane working at cohort scope.

### 2.3 The cross-tenant employee-discount referral

On day 14, Reza (who now works at a company called Coda) posts: "Coda has an employee-referral program. If a cohort member applies and gets hired, I get a referral bonus and you get a smoother process. Here's the structured referral link." 

The structured-referral link is a Community → Coda cross-tenant intent envelope. When clicked, it:
- Pre-fills Chris's application context (his Community LinkedIn-mode badge, his verified-former-employer attestation from this cohort, his portfolio link from j143).
- Attaches Reza's referral-of-record.
- Routes the application through Coda's HR with the referral-bonus flag.

Two cohort members apply via Reza's referral. One gets hired (Olu, the DevOps engineer from Lagos). Reza receives a $5,000 referral bonus from Coda; he splits half with Olu privately ("you would have applied without me; I'm just glad to share"). The cohort cheers in #commiseration ("congrats Olu!"). Audit emits the referral-bonus flow.

## Chapter 3 — The cohort as a long-lived primitive

### 3.1 What happens at T+90d (the 30-day-revocation tail)

At T+30d (2026-06-26), former-employer tenant fully revokes Chris's work-tenant access (j142 final). But his **alumni** sub-tenant membership persists. The verified-former-employer attestation is signed once and stored in audit-chain forever — even if the former-employer tenant ceased operations.

The cohort channel persists too. Even when the former-employer's main tenant is no longer paying for it (it's a separate billing line — Marcus pays a small fee to host the alumni sub-tenant for 12 months as part of the RIF severance package; after 12 months, the cohort can elect to take over hosting themselves via a community-co-op tenant).

### 3.2 At T+12mo: cohort transitions to community-co-op

In 2027-05, the cohort hosting transitions. Marcus's tenant stops paying. The cohort holds an election:
- Continue with rotating-volunteer moderation? (passes 142-23-32)
- Pay collectively for hosting (estimated $200/year all-in)? (passes 165-18-14)

The cohort sub-tenant becomes a community-co-op-tenant — owned by the 200 members, governed by a 5-member elected council. Audit emits `CohortGovernanceTransition{from=employer_hosted, to=community_co_op}`.

This is, in oyatie terms, the canonical demonstration of a **long-lived alumni cohort that survives the original tenant**.

## Chapter 4 — Why this story matters

j147 demonstrates:

1. **Verified-former-employer is a cryptographic primitive.** Not a self-claim; not a LinkedIn-honor-system. Signed by HR-admin key; verifiable by anyone with public key.
2. **The cohort is more than a Slack channel.** It's a Cedar-gated sub-tenant with member-elected moderators and proper audit trails.
3. **Cross-tenant referrals are structured.** No off-platform DM-to-recruiter chaos. The referral envelope carries verified context; the receiving employer can trust the chain.
4. **Anti-fraud at cohort scope** uses the same detection-substrate as personal-tenant (HRRP signals) — economies of scale.
5. **The cohort survives the original tenant.** Identity attestation is signed once, persists forever. Hosting transitions democratically.

## Chapter 6 — Open questions

1. Should the verified-former-employer attestation expire? (No — the attestation is a historical statement of fact; never expires; but it can be augmented by a "current employment status" attestation separately.)
2. What if the former-employer's HR-admin key gets compromised? (Per ADR-0299, key rotation triggers re-signing of valid attestations; revoked attestations marked accordingly.)
3. What about cohorts for collective bargaining? (j147 is about mutual aid; collective-bargaining would be a separate journey under NLRA pack.)
4. What if the cohort wants to fund a class-action lawsuit collectively? (Out of scope; possible via Marketplace's bespoke-service primitive + escrow for legal-services payment.)
