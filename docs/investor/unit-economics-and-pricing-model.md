# Unit Economics and Pricing Model

## 01 - Model Purpose

- This document defines Oyatie's investor-facing pricing and unit-economics model.
- The model is a planning artifact, not audited financial history.
- The model is designed for seed-stage diligence.
- The model ties pricing to tenant value, capability tier, regulated posture, and usage.
- The model avoids pricing as a generic per-seat SaaS clone.
- The model assumes Oyatie sells consolidation, governance, evidence, and workflow execution.
- The model uses tenant ACV as the primary subscription unit.
- The model uses capability-tier activation as the main expansion lever.
- The model uses usage-based metering for AI, workflow execution, integration calls, and evidence volume.
- The model uses deployment premiums for dedicated cells and regulated environments.
- The model uses migration services as one-time revenue or partner-led revenue.
- The model targets 78 percent subscription gross margin at scale.
- The model accepts 55 to 65 percent gross margin during early pilots.
- The model targets CAC payback below 18 months by year 3.
- The model targets LTV/CAC above 4.0x by year 3.
- The model targets net revenue retention above 130 percent by year 3.
- The model targets enterprise gross retention above 90 percent.
- The model expects enterprise sales cycles of 6 to 9 months in the base case.
- The model expects SMB sales cycles of 60 to 120 days after onboarding is repeatable.
- The model assumes early enterprise contracts receive discounts up to 40 percent.
- The model assumes list pricing becomes more disciplined after the first 6 enterprise customers.
- The model assumes customer engineering is a bottleneck before product-led onboarding.
- The model assumes cloud and AI costs are actively controlled by budget gates.
- The model treats pricing as a moat only when tied to measurable buyer value.

## 02 - Pricing Principles

- Principle 1: price the tenant, not only the seat.
- Principle 2: price the governed capability tier, not a disconnected module.
- Principle 3: price regulated deployment separately from standard managed cloud.
- Principle 4: price evidence generation as recurring value.
- Principle 5: price AI and workflow usage with hard budget ceilings.
- Principle 6: price migration services separately from subscription ARR.
- Principle 7: price partner implementation outside core gross-margin targets.
- Principle 8: preserve transparent downgrade and upgrade paths.
- Principle 9: avoid hidden AI overage surprises.
- Principle 10: expose cost attribution per tenant and capability.
- Principle 12: let compliance packs force higher tiers when evidence depth requires it.
- Principle 13: keep SMB pricing simple.
- Principle 14: keep enterprise pricing anchored to replacement or containment value.
- Principle 15: keep dedicated cell pricing high enough to protect margin.
- Principle 16: offer design-partner discounts only for reference rights and feedback obligations.
- Principle 17: require executive sponsor for discounted enterprise pilots.
- Principle 19: do not sell broad vertical transformation before the wedge works.
- Principle 20: every price should map to a cost driver and a value driver.
- Principle 21: price annual upfront by default.
- Principle 22: offer multi-year discounts only after gross margin is understood.
- Principle 23: benchmark against Salesforce, SAP, Workday, ServiceNow, Microsoft, and Palantir alternatives.
- Principle 24: document every exception in a pricing approval log.

## 10 - Usage-Based Pricing

- AI invocation usage is billed after included allowance.
- Workflow execution usage is billed after included allowance.
- Evidence volume usage is billed after included allowance.
- Integration call usage is billed after included allowance.
- Storage usage is billed after included allowance.
- Overage unit 1: AI provider cost plus 100 percent markup in early phase.
- Overage unit 2: workflow execution at $0.002 per execution above allowance.
- Overage unit 3: evidence events at $0.10 per 1,000 events above allowance.
- Overage unit 4: integration calls at $0.20 per 1,000 calls above allowance.
- Overage unit 5: managed storage at $0.35 per GB-month above allowance.
- Overage unit 6: high-retention archive at $0.08 per GB-month.
- Overage unit 7: audit retrieval job at $25 per 100,000 records exported.
- Overage unit 8: premium connector transaction at $0.001 per transaction.

- AI usage gross margin target early: 50 percent.
- AI usage gross margin target at scale: 65 percent.
- Workflow usage gross margin target at scale: 80 percent.
- Evidence usage gross margin target at scale: 80 percent.
- Cost ceiling is required for every tenant.
- Budget alert threshold: 50 percent, 80 percent, 95 percent, and 100 percent of committed usage budget.
- Cedar budget-deny action triggers at 100 percent unless customer authorizes overage.
- Zylo reports AI and consumption pricing surprises; Oyatie should position transparent ceilings as a trust feature.
- Usage pricing competitor names: OpenAI API, Anthropic API, Microsoft Copilot consumption patterns, Salesforce AI credit models, ServiceNow AI agents.
- The pricing strategy is to monetize usage without surprising customers.

- CAC formula: sales and marketing spend plus allocated sales engineering cost per acquired customer.
- CAC payback formula: CAC divided by monthly gross profit.
- LTV/CAC formula: lifetime gross profit divided by CAC.
- Enterprise annual gross profit target: $900,000.

- Enterprise base LTV: $4,500,000.
- Enterprise CAC target: $360,000.
- Enterprise LTV/CAC target: 12.5x.

- Enterprise conservative LTV: $2,700,000.
- Enterprise conservative LTV/CAC: 7.5x.
- SMB annual gross profit target: $56,000.
- SMB expected lifetime base: 3 years.
- SMB base LTV: $168,000.
- SMB CAC target: $20,000.
- SMB LTV/CAC target: 8.4x.
- SMB conservative lifetime: 2 years.
- SMB conservative LTV: $112,000.
- SMB conservative LTV/CAC: 5.6x.

## 26 - Pricing Conclusion

- Oyatie should price as a governed enterprise operating substrate.
- Oyatie should not price as a simple seat-based productivity tool.
- Evidence packs create recurring compliance value.
- Dedicated cells create regulated deployment value.
- Usage pricing captures AI and workflow activity but must be capped.
- Migration services are necessary but should move to partners.
- Unit economics are attractive only if product proof reduces services load.
- Gross margin is most sensitive to AI cost, dedicated cell cost, and customer engineering load.
- CAC is most sensitive to enterprise sales cycle and security review friction.
- NRR is most sensitive to second-workflow expansion.
- LTV/CAC can be excellent if enterprise retention holds.
- LTV/CAC can collapse if pilots remain bespoke.
- The $18.0 million seed plan must fund productization, not unlimited implementation.
- The first paid pilot by 2026-12-15 is the unit-economics starting line.
- The $1.8 million ARR milestone by 2027-05-31 is the first pricing proof.
- The $6.0 million ARR milestone by 2027-12-31 is the first repeatability proof.
- The $24.0 million base ARR milestone by 2029-05-31 is the first scale proof.
- The $60.0 million upside ARR milestone by 2029-05-31 is the venture acceleration case.
