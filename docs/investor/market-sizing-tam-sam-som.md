# Market Sizing: TAM, SAM, SOM

## 01 - Scope and Method

- This document sizes Oyatie as a B2B SaaS replacement and consolidation platform.
- The market is not sized as a single application category.
- The market is sized as the spend pool exposed by fragmented enterprise software categories.
- The TAM frame covers replaceable or containable B2B SaaS and enterprise workflow software.
- The SAM frame narrows to multinational and regulated organizations with acute governance needs.
- The SOM frame narrows to year-3 reachable ARR with founder-led enterprise sales and focused product delivery.
- The analysis uses public market data from Gartner, Forrester, and Zylo.
- The analysis uses internal architecture facts from the Oyatie repository.
- The market model is investor-facing and should be reconciled against a spreadsheet before fundraising.
- Dollar values are U.S. dollars unless otherwise noted.
- Gartner's April 2026 IT forecast is used as the top-down spending anchor.
- Gartner's November 2024 public cloud forecast is used as the SaaS cloud anchor.
- Gartner's July 2025 enterprise business applications market map is used as the business-applications anchor.
- Gartner's June 2025 CRM market share abstract is used as the CRM anchor.
- Forrester's February 2026 global technology forecast is used as the enterprise and government spend-growth anchor.
- Zylo's 2026 SaaS Management Index is used as the average portfolio spend and app-count anchor.
- Internal repository counts are used only as product-depth evidence.
- Internal repository counts are not used to claim market adoption.
- TAM is modeled as $900.0 billion.
- SAM is modeled as $110.0 billion.
- Year-3 SOM is modeled as $60.0 million ARR upside and $24.0 million ARR base case.
- The model intentionally discounts Gartner's $1.444 trillion 2026 software spend to avoid overclaiming.
- The model treats infrastructure-only and consumer software as outside core TAM.
- The model treats professional services implementation spend as indirect opportunity, not core ARR.
- The model treats migration services as a separate one-time revenue line.
- The model excludes advertising media spend.
- The model excludes consumer social and gaming spend.
- The model excludes pure cloud compute resale.
- The model includes enterprise applications, workflow, collaboration, compliance, integration, data, and AI-governed operations.
- The sizing conclusion is that market availability is not the gating risk.
- The gating risk is execution, trust, and focused commercialization.

## 03 - TAM Definition

- TAM means total annual budget pool for B2B SaaS replacement, containment, and governance consolidation.
- TAM includes CRM, ERP-adjacent workflow, HCM-adjacent workflow, ITSM, collaboration, analytics, compliance, integration, workflow automation, AI governance, and cloud operations software.
- TAM includes spend that Oyatie could replace directly.
- TAM includes spend that Oyatie could contain by becoming the governed workflow and evidence layer around incumbents.
- TAM includes spend that Oyatie could influence through capability packs and marketplace revenue.
- TAM excludes hyperscaler infrastructure resale.
- TAM excludes implementation services by systems integrators.
- TAM excludes consumer subscriptions.
- TAM excludes unmanaged one-off productivity tools below enterprise governance threshold.
- Top-down anchor 1 is Gartner's $1.444 trillion 2026 software spend.
- Top-down anchor 2 is Gartner's $299.071 billion 2025 SaaS public cloud application services spend.
- Top-down anchor 3 is Gartner's $254 billion 2025 enterprise business applications market.
- Top-down anchor 4 is Forrester's $5.6 trillion 2026 global technology spend.
- Top-down anchor 5 is Zylo's $55.7 million average SaaS portfolio spend.
- Internal TAM model starts with $1.444 trillion software spend.
- Internal TAM model removes approximately $300 billion for consumer, developer-only, security-only, and infrastructure-adjacent software outside near-term scope.
- Internal TAM model removes approximately $160 billion for categories that are not plausible near-term replacement or containment targets.
- Internal TAM model adds back approximately $60 billion for AI governance and workflow budget adjacent to IT services.
- Internal TAM result is $900.0 billion.
- The $900.0 billion TAM is a modeled opportunity pool.
- The $900.0 billion TAM is not a forecasted Oyatie revenue opportunity.
- The $900.0 billion TAM is large enough to support venture-scale outcomes.
- The TAM model should be stress-tested against category-level reports before external fundraising.
- The useful investor message is "large, expanding, and budgeted."
- The useful buyer message is "we target spend you already have."
- The useful operating message is "do not build for the whole TAM first."
- The first product wedge addresses a fraction of TAM through regulated workflow and evidence.
- The first market proof is signed ARR, not total market charts.
- The TAM answer is sufficient if an investor believes enterprise software consolidation is real.

## 04 - SAM Definition

- SAM means serviceable available market for the next 5 to 7 years.
- SAM narrows TAM to organizations where Oyatie's specific strengths matter.
- SAM prioritizes regulated industries.
- SAM prioritizes multinational operations.
- SAM prioritizes complex SaaS portfolios.
- SAM prioritizes AI governance pressure.
- SAM prioritizes audit-chain and evidence-pack pain.
- SAM prioritizes deployment portability and sovereign-cell needs.
- SAM excludes customers who only want a cheap point solution.
- SAM excludes customers who cannot buy platform software.
- SAM excludes customers whose workflow complexity is low.
- SAM excludes customers unwilling to adopt new enterprise substrates.
- SAM includes financial services.
- SAM includes healthcare and life sciences.
- SAM includes public sector contractors.
- SAM includes regulated manufacturing.
- SAM includes logistics and supply chain operators.
- SAM includes professional services and legal/accounting networks.
- SAM includes enterprise SaaS companies under customer trust pressure.
- SAM includes multinational midmarket companies.
- SAM includes AI-forward organizations with compliance concerns.
- SAM model applies a 12.2 percent serviceability factor to $900.0 billion TAM.
- SAM result is $110.0 billion.
- SAM assumes initial geographic focus in United States, South Korea, European Union, Japan, Singapore, Australia, and Canada.
- SAM assumes compliance packs for SOC 2, ISO 27001, GDPR, HIPAA, KR-PIPA, KR-FSS, DORA, and EU AI Act become commercial assets.
- SAM assumes Kubernetes-first deployment portability matters to a subset of buyers.
- SAM assumes Oyatie can sell containment before full replacement.
- SAM assumes a partner channel emerges by 2028.
- SAM assumes cloud marketplace procurement becomes an accelerator after 2028-06-30.
- SAM is still too broad for execution planning.
- SOM defines the actual near-term target.

## 05 - SOM Definition

- SOM means year-3 serviceable obtainable market.
- SOM is modeled as ARR reachable by 2029-05-31.
- Base-case SOM is $24.0 million ARR.
- Upside SOM is $60.0 million ARR.
- Base case assumes 12 enterprise tenants at $1.2 million ARR.
- Base case assumes 120 SMB or midmarket tenants at $80,000 ARR.
- Base case assumes $14.4 million enterprise ARR.
- Base case assumes $9.6 million SMB and midmarket ARR.
- Upside case assumes 30 enterprise tenants at $1.2 million ARR.
- Upside case assumes 300 SMB or midmarket tenants at $80,000 ARR.
- Upside case assumes $36.0 million enterprise ARR.
- Upside case assumes $24.0 million SMB and midmarket ARR.
- Year-3 enterprise tenant count is intentionally small.
- Year-3 SMB tenant count assumes repeatable onboarding exists.
- Year-3 ACV assumes capability-tier expansion.
- Year-3 model excludes one-time migration services.
- Year-3 model excludes marketplace take-rate revenue.
- Year-3 model excludes professional services pass-through.
- Year-3 model excludes speculative usage upside.
- Year-3 model includes deployment premiums only for enterprise tenants.
- Year-3 model assumes 130 percent net revenue retention target.
- Year-3 model assumes enterprise gross retention above 90 percent.
- Year-3 model assumes sales capacity from 6 enterprise sellers by late 2028.
- Year-3 model assumes customer engineering can onboard 2 enterprise tenants per quarter by 2028.
- Year-3 model assumes seed-funded proof unlocks Series A by 2027-08-31.
- Year-3 model assumes no broad vertical fanout before shared substrate proof.
- SOM is a sales execution target, not a market availability claim.
- The investor diligence question is whether $24.0 million to $60.0 million ARR by year 3 is credible.
- Credibility depends on product proof by 2026-09-30.
- Credibility depends on first paid pilot by 2026-12-15.
- Credibility depends on first contracted ARR by 2027-01-31.
