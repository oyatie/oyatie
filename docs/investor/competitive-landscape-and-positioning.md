# Competitive Landscape and Positioning

## 01 - Positioning Thesis

- Oyatie competes for the enterprise operating-system budget, not only the SaaS application budget.
- The competitive frame is unified ecosystem versus fragmented best-of-breed.
- The first buyer comparison is against Salesforce, SAP, Workday, ServiceNow, Microsoft, Oracle, AWS, Google Cloud, and Azure.
- The second buyer comparison is against vertical SaaS companies that win one workflow at a time.
- The third buyer comparison is against internal platform engineering teams.
- The fourth buyer comparison is against consulting-led transformation programs.
- Oyatie's thesis is that shared substrate primitives can make application categories composable.
- The platform does not need to displace every incumbent at once.
- The platform needs to land in workflows where governance cost exceeds point-solution convenience.
- The best wedge is regulated operational workflow plus evidence generation.
- The strongest economic argument is SaaS consolidation plus policy proof.
- The strongest technical argument is substrate depth across identity, policy, workflow, audit, quota, data, and deployment.
- The strongest strategic argument is that fragmented SaaS suites are converging into AI-governed execution platforms.
- The largest incumbents are already validating platform consolidation.
- Salesforce is bundling CRM, data cloud, Slack, MuleSoft, Tableau, and AI agents.
- Microsoft is bundling Teams, Dynamics, Power Platform, Microsoft 365, Azure, Copilot, and Entra.
- SAP is bundling ERP, SuccessFactors, Ariba, BTP, analytics, and Joule.
- Workday is expanding from HCM into finance, planning, procurement, analytics, and AI.
- ServiceNow is expanding from ITSM into enterprise workflows, CRM, HR, security, and AI agents.
- AWS, Google Cloud, and Azure are selling the infrastructure substrate but leave business application semantics fragmented.
- Vertical SaaS vendors win depth but usually inherit fragmented enterprise identity, data, and evidence models.
- Oyatie's wedge is not being more famous than incumbents.
- Oyatie's wedge is having one governed substrate where incumbents expose many partially overlapping platforms.
- The investor diligence question is whether that wedge can convert into a category-level platform.
