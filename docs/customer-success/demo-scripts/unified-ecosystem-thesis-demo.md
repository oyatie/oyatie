## Opening Hook

- Say: "The enterprise software market trained companies to buy a new app for every problem."
- Say: "One for CRM."
- Say: "One for ERP."
- Say: "One for procurement."
- Say: "One for GRC."
- Say: "One for workflow."
- Say: "One for integration."
- Say: "One for analytics."
- Say: "One for AI."
- Say: "One for every region, department, and exception."
- Say: "At first, that feels like speed."
- Say: "Then the operating model fractures."
- Say: "The customer record is in one place."
- Say: "The supplier risk is in another."
- Say: "The approval is in a workflow tool."
- Say: "The policy decision is in IAM."
- Say: "The evidence is in GRC."
- Say: "The cost is in finance."
- Say: "The board asks one question and five teams assemble a narrative by hand."
- Say: "This is the 47 SaaS app problem."
- Say: "The number is not literal for every company."
- Say: "The pattern is."
- Say: "Oyatie's thesis is that the future enterprise does not need another narrow app island."
- Say: "It needs a unified operating ecosystem."
- Say: "That ecosystem needs tenant boundaries, capability tiers, policy, workflow, evidence, marketplace settlement, and cost visibility built into one substrate."
- Say: "This does not mean big-bang replacement."
- Say: "It means entering through one high-value operating wedge, proving the pattern, and expanding capability by capability."
- Say: "Today I will use the Helios Fortune 500 manufacturing fixture."
- Say: "It is a synthetic global enterprise with plants, suppliers, compliance obligations, ERP parity mapping, procurement, workflow, and evidence."
- Say: "We will start with a map of 47 synthetic SaaS systems."
- Say: "Then we will collapse the operating story into one tenant substrate."
- Say: "We will show how ERP replacement becomes capability-tier migration, not a monolith swap."
- Say: "We will show how compliance packs travel with work."
- Say: "We will show how workflows, approvals, and evidence share one policy fabric."
- Say: "We will show an economic model with dollars."
- Say: "The concrete first-year example is an 84 million dollar annual SaaS, integration, and workflow-sprawl base."
- Say: "We will not claim all of that disappears."
- Say: "A credible first-year target might be 8 to 14 million dollars of value from rationalized tools, reduced manual evidence, faster cycle time, and lower integration burden."
- Say: "The decision after this session is not whether to replace everything."
- Say: "The decision is whether a C-suite ecosystem workshop should identify the first wedge and quantify the 18-month roadmap."
- Pause.
- Ask: "For this conversation, should we bias toward CFO value, CIO architecture, COO operating model, or CEO strategic optionality?"
- Adjust screen sequence based on answer.

## Discovery Questions

1. How many SaaS applications does the executive team believe are strategic versus accidental?
2. Which business process exposes the most painful fragmentation across systems?
3. Where does the board ask for answers that require manual cross-system assembly?
4. What is annual spend across SaaS subscriptions, integration, consulting, workflow tools, and data reconciliation?
5. Which enterprise systems are untouchable for the next 24 months?
6. Which systems are already viewed as consolidation candidates?
7. Which operating capability matters most: procurement, manufacturing operations, revenue, compliance, finance, AI governance, or customer operations?
8. How much autonomy must regional teams and business units retain?
9. Where do compliance obligations block transformation today?
10. Which workflow has both high executive pain and measurable economics?
11. How are AI initiatives governed across business units today?
12. What is the current integration architecture: point-to-point, iPaaS, event bus, custom middleware, or mixed?
13. Which executive owns the transformation value case?
14. What would make the board believe this is an ecosystem strategy rather than another platform purchase?
15. What must be proven in 90 days to justify an 18-month expansion roadmap?

## Objection Handling

1. Objection: "This sounds like boiling the ocean."
- Response name: Wedge-First.
- Say: "The thesis is broad, but execution starts with one measurable wedge."
- Say: "The roadmap expands only after value and governance are proven."
- Say: "We avoid big-bang transformation."

2. Objection: "We just bought a major suite."
- Response name: Suite-Coexistence.
- Say: "The first question is which suite capabilities are strategic."
- Say: "Oyatie can wrap cross-system workflows and evidence without replacing strategic systems immediately."
- Say: "The workshop identifies coexistence and retirement boundaries."

3. Objection: "ERP replacement is too risky."
- Response name: ERP-Parity-By-Capability.
- Say: "We do not start by replacing the ERP core."
- Say: "We map ERP capabilities, run dry migrations, and select operating wedges."
- Say: "Replacement is earned by evidence."

4. Objection: "Our business units need autonomy."
- Response name: Autonomy-Within-Substrate.
- Say: "Autonomy is preserved through tenant, region, role, and capability configuration."
- Say: "Shared substrate does not mean identical local process."
- Say: "It means governance and evidence are consistent where they must be."

5. Objection: "We cannot quantify app sprawl."
- Response name: Portfolio-Heatmap.
- Say: "The workshop builds the app, integration, workflow, and evidence heatmap."
- Say: "We separate strategic systems from accidental duplication."
- Say: "That becomes the CFO-facing baseline."

6. Objection: "This will take years."
- Response name: Ninety-Day-Proof.
- Say: "The enterprise roadmap may run 18 months."
- Say: "The first proof should be 90 days or less."
- Say: "If a wedge cannot show value quickly, it should not become a program."

7. Objection: "Our architecture team will resist another platform."
- Response name: Architecture-Led-Workshop.
- Say: "Architecture should co-own the workshop."
- Say: "The first deliverable is boundaries: reads, writes, events, policy, evidence, and retirement candidates."
- Say: "Without that, we should not proceed."

8. Objection: "We need AI, not workflow."
- Response name: AI-Needs-Operating-Rails.
- Say: "AI without workflow and policy becomes another layer of sprawl."
- Say: "The demo showed AI draft assistance inside governed workflow."
- Say: "That is how AI becomes operational."

9. Objection: "We need workflow, not a platform."
- Response name: Workflow-Is-The-Entry.
- Say: "Workflow is often the right entry point."
- Say: "The reason to use Oyatie is that workflow comes with policy, evidence, capability tiers, and cost visibility."
- Say: "That prevents the next workflow island."

10. Objection: "Our CFO will not accept soft savings."
- Response name: Assumption-Led-Value.
- Say: "We separate hard savings, productivity, risk reduction, and strategic optionality."
- Say: "The workshop assigns owners and assumptions to each value lever."
- Say: "Only validated levers go into the pilot case."

11. Objection: "We cannot retire systems because contracts are locked."
- Response name: Contract-Aware-Roadmap.
- Say: "Contract dates become roadmap constraints."
- Say: "Early value can come from evidence automation, workflow consolidation, and integration reduction before license retirement."
- Say: "We do not need every contract to end immediately."

12. Objection: "The board has seen platform promises before."
- Response name: Board-Wedge-Evidence.
- Say: "The board should not approve a platform promise."
- Say: "It should approve a staged wedge with measurable outputs."
- Say: "The board pack makes success criteria explicit."

13. Objection: "Regional compliance rules will make this impossible."
- Response name: Pack-And-Tenant-Composition.
- Say: "Regional compliance becomes pack and tenant configuration."
- Say: "The parent gets aggregate visibility where permitted."
- Say: "Local restrictions remain enforceable."

14. Objection: "We have an iPaaS already."
- Response name: Integration-Is-Not-Operating-Truth.
- Say: "An iPaaS moves data."
- Say: "Oyatie coordinates capability, policy, workflow, evidence, and cost."
- Say: "They can coexist."

15. Objection: "Custom build gives us more control."
- Response name: Build-Only-If-You-Want-To-Own-The-Category.
- Say: "Custom build may be right for a narrow differentiating process."
- Say: "The risk is rebuilding workflow, policy, evidence, tenancy, and governance from scratch."
- Say: "Oyatie is for teams that want productized substrate plus configuration."

16. Objection: "This threatens existing transformation programs."
- Response name: Program-Alignment.
- Say: "The workshop should map existing programs and decide where Oyatie accelerates or stays out."
- Say: "We should not duplicate active investments."
- Say: "The goal is program leverage, not political conflict."

17. Objection: "Our data is too messy."
- Response name: Messy-Data-Is-The-Reason.
- Say: "Messy data is normal."
- Say: "The first wedge should use enough data to prove operating value without requiring a full enterprise data cleanup."
- Say: "The roadmap then improves data quality where value justifies it."

18. Objection: "What if the first wedge fails?"
- Response name: Reversible-Pilot.
- Say: "A good wedge is bounded and reversible."
- Say: "Failure should produce a useful map of systems, controls, and economics."
- Say: "That is still valuable if the pilot is scoped correctly."

19. Objection: "How do we govern ownership?"
