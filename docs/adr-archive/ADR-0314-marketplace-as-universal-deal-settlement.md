# ADR-0314: Marketplace as Universal Deal-Settlement Substrate

## Status

Proposed - 2026-05-20. This ADR extends ADR-0249 from multi-category marketplace surface doctrine into the deeper settlement doctrine needed for ERP parity and cross-tenant commerce.

## §A Context

Marketplace is no longer only a catalog surface. In oyatie it is the universal deal-settlement substrate: every commercial agreement between two tenants, or between a tenant and a consumer, lands as a deal set with priced terms, counterparties, entitlements, obligations, settlement rails, audit-chain evidence, and policy gates.
The doctrine covers goods, services, subscriptions, capability grants, plugins, workforce contracts, M&A transitions, joint ventures, data licenses, receivables assignments, usage-based commitments, supplier agreements, and customer-specific enterprise contracts.
The hyperscaler pattern is already visible in SAP Ariba for procurement networks, Stripe for platform-facilitated money movement, Coupa for spend management, and Salesforce Commerce Cloud for enterprise commerce experiences. oyatie adopts the settlement substrate pattern while preserving flat microservice ownership per ADR-0131 and no-grouping boundaries per ADR-0132.
Tenant identity remains the universal scoping primitive per ADR-0244. A deal never floats outside tenant scope; even a consumer purchase is `tenant + consumer_principal`, and a conglomerate deal uses sovereign child tenants plus controlling-entity grants per ADR-0313.

### §A.1 ERP reason this ADR exists
SAP S/4HANA parity cannot be achieved if marketplace only means retail catalog. FI, CO, MM, SD, SRM, CRM, GTS, TRM, EWM, and network products all need deal settlement as a common primitive. Purchase requisitions, sales orders, subscriptions, supplier contracts, freight contracts, lease contracts, field-service contracts, app-store purchases, contingent-worker contracts, and receivables assignments are all deal sets with different overlays.

### §A.2 Coverage boundary
This ADR does not create an ERP-suite microservice. It defines the settlement primitive and the action namespaces that ERP-facing microservices use. Domain ownership remains distributed across marketplace, payments, finops-portal, workflow-engine, ontology, connect, treasury, warehouse, global-trade, crm, and other flat microservices.

## §B Decision

oyatie will model marketplace as the universal deal-settlement substrate. Every tenant-to-tenant and tenant-to-consumer commercial exchange must be expressible as a `DealSet` rooted in tenant scope, Cedar-gated at every action, projected into ontology object types, and settled through payments/treasury/finops as appropriate.

### §B.1 Required primitive
A `DealSet` is the canonical envelope for offer, acceptance, obligation, entitlement, settlement, dispute, revocation, amendment, and renewal. A DealSet can represent a retail order, a procurement contract, a services milestone, a plugin entitlement, a subscription, a data license, a workforce engagement, a receivable sale, a merger transition service agreement, or a joint-venture capital call.

### §B.2 Mandatory participants
A DealSet always has at least one seller-side tenant, one buyer-side tenant or consumer principal, one settlement policy, one audit-chain stream, one ontology object type, one workflow template, and one Cedar policy fragment. Multi-party deals add `counterparty_roles[]` rather than creating parallel deal tables.

### §B.3 Rejected alternatives
- Rejected: one deal table per ERP module. Reason: duplicates settlement semantics and breaks cross-module auditability.
- Rejected: a monolithic commerce/ERP platform. Reason: violates ADR-0132 and forces unrelated ERP domains to share deployment cadence.
- Rejected: payments-only settlement. Reason: ERP parity requires non-money obligations, entitlements, data licenses, audit evidence, service levels, and post-settlement controls.
- Rejected: unscoped external marketplace accounts. Reason: violates ADR-0244 tenant scoping and breaks DSAR, audit, and cost attribution.

## §C Consequences

| Engineering-rigor dimension | Consequence | Acceptance signal |
|---|---|---|
| Maintainability | Settlement logic is one primitive and domain overlays stay in flat microservices. | DealSet schema versioned; overlays named; reverse dependencies listed. |
| Observability | Every settlement transition emits audit events, metrics, logs, and traces. | `oya_marketplace_deal_transition_total`, `DealSetAccepted`, `DealSetSettled`, and span links present. |

| Code quality | Contracts ship as OpenAPI 3.2.0, AsyncAPI 3.1.0, and proto3, with property and replay tests. | BNF v4.1 naming and ADR-0105 13-layer mapping pass. |

## §D Detailed Mechanics

### §D-1 DealSet object model
DealSet fields: `deal_set_id`, `tenant_scope`, `counterparty_roles`, `deal_category`, `commercial_terms`, `obligation_terms`, `entitlement_terms`, `settlement_terms`, `tax_terms`, `trade_terms`, `data_terms`, `workforce_terms`, `audit_chain_ref`, `ontology_object_ref`, `workflow_run_ref`, `cedar_policy_ref`, `version`, `status`, and `effective_window`.
Every field has a tenant-scoped owner. Cross-tenant read access is granted through Cedar, not by copying rows into another tenant's namespace.

### §D-2 Deal categories
| Category | Examples | Owning overlay | Settlement rail |
|---|---|---|---|
| goods | retail order, inventory purchase, spare part | marketplace + warehouse | payments + tax + global-trade |
| services | milestone work, support contract, field service | marketplace + workflow-engine + crm | escrow + milestone release |
| subscriptions | SaaS seat, content subscription, maintenance plan | payments + marketplace | recurring billing |
| capability grants | plugin entitlement, API quota, tenant capability | plugin-app-store + developer-sdk | entitlement ledger |
| workforce contracts | contractor, contingent labor, fieldglass-style engagement | workplace-integration + marketplace | payroll or payables |
| corporate transactions | M&A, joint venture, transition services agreement | tenancy + marketplace + finops | treasury + audit-chain |
| data licenses | analytics dataset, model feature, benchmark corpus | ontology + marketplace + intelligence | license ledger + payment |
| receivables | invoice factoring, assignment, pledge | treasury + finops-portal + payments | treasury rail |

### §D-3 Action namespaces
`marketplace.deal.offer.create`, `marketplace.deal.offer.publish`, `marketplace.deal.accept`, `marketplace.deal.amend`, `marketplace.deal.revoke`, `marketplace.deal.settle`, `marketplace.deal.dispute.open`, `marketplace.deal.dispute.resolve`, `marketplace.deal.entitlement.grant`, `marketplace.deal.entitlement.revoke`, `marketplace.deal.receivable.assign`, and `marketplace.deal.joint_venture.bind` are reserved action namespaces.

### §D-4 Cedar gate shape
Every action evaluates `principal`, `action`, `resource: DealSet`, and `context` with tenant scope, counterparty role, data class, jurisdiction pack, cell tier, delegated authority, and audit-chain target. Default deny applies.

### §D-5 Contracts
REST surfaces use OpenAPI 3.2.0. Event surfaces use AsyncAPI 3.1.0. Internal service-to-service contracts use proto3. All names follow BNF v4.1 and every implementation crate maps to an ADR-0105 13-layer enum.

### §D-6 Relation to SAP and ERP modules
SAP Ariba-like procurement, Coupa-like spend control, Stripe Connect-like platform payouts, Salesforce Commerce Cloud-like commerce, and SAP Commerce Cloud-like catalog flows all converge on DealSet. FI/CO sees accounting events; MM sees purchase requisitions and goods receipts; SD sees sales orders and billing; SRM sees supplier performance; CRM sees account and loyalty events; GTS sees customs and export-control events.

### §D-7 Failure-mode tree
- Counterparty revokes authority during acceptance: state remains `acceptance_pending`; no settlement event emits; both tenants receive audit-chain evidence.
- PSP outage during settlement: DealSet moves to `settlement_deferred`; entitlement release follows the configured risk policy; retry schedule is visible.
- Sanctions screening hit after acceptance: global-trade places `trade_hold`; payments/treasury rails pause; workflow-engine opens investigation.
- Region unreachable: home-cell writes queue locally; cross-region replica is eventually consistent; user-facing state names the stale-region condition.
- Key compromise: OpenBao secret reference rotates; every deal action after compromise requires step-up authorization.

### §D-8 Capacity and performance model
Little's Law applies to deal acceptance: `concurrency = arrival_rate * service_time`. At 2,000 accept attempts per second and 250 ms p95 service time, the acceptance tier needs at least 500 active workers before headroom. We target 2x headroom, so baseline production admission starts at 1,000 worker-equivalent slots partitioned by tenant and cell.

## §E Implementation Footprint

- `microservices/marketplace/` owns DealSet authoring, category catalog, offer publication, counterparty negotiation, and entitlement projection.
- `microservices/payments/` owns payment authorization, capture, refund, payout, escrow release, and PSP routing.
- `microservices/treasury/` owns cash position, debt, FX, hedge, receivables assignment, and liquidity evidence.
- `microservices/finops-portal/` owns invoice presentation, chargeback, cost allocation, credits, and FOCUS export.
- `microservices/ontology/` owns DealSet object types, relation types, and tenant subclassing.
- `microservices/workflow-engine/` owns lifecycle workflow execution, approvals, disputes, renewals, and amendment cascades.
- `microservices/connector/` owns SAP, Oracle, Workday, NetSuite, bank, tax, carrier, and compliance adapter ingestion.
- `microservices/global-trade/` owns sanctions, customs, export control, and trade hold decisions.

## §F Migration

Existing marketplace order, subscription, plugin, and payment surfaces migrate by wrapping their current order IDs in DealSet envelopes. No data is silently merged across tenants. The first migration wave adds dual-write DealSet projections, the second wave flips read paths, and the third wave retires bespoke settlement tables after audit-chain reconciliation.
SAP Ariba, Coupa, Salesforce Commerce Cloud, Stripe Connect, Oracle, Workday, NetSuite, and custom ERP exports enter through connect adapters and produce DealSet import batches with row-level provenance.

## §H Change Log and Naming Justifications

- 2026-05-20: Proposed alongside ADR-0315 ERP coverage doctrine.
- `DealSet`: chosen because one deal can include multiple offers, line items, obligations, entitlements, and settlement rails. Rejected `Order` because services, data licenses, workforce contracts, and M&A obligations are not always orders.
- `marketplace.deal.*`: chosen as action namespace because marketplace is the settlement substrate and deal is the universal object. Rejected `commerce.*` because it underplays procurement, receivables, workforce, and corporate transactions.
- `EVT-DEALSET-*`: chosen as audit-event prefix because audit-chain readers can filter every settlement transition without knowing the ERP module.

### §D-X Deal primitive: retail-order
- Settlement doctrine: retail-order is represented as a DealSet category overlay, never as an isolated settlement table.
- Hyperscaler precedent: SAP Ariba or Coupa covers procurement/spend shape; Stripe or Salesforce Commerce Cloud covers platform-facilitated settlement shape as applicable.
- Tenant rule: every retail-order row carries tenant scope, counterparty role, region, compliance pack, and audit-chain stream.
- Cedar rule: create, accept, amend, settle, dispute, and revoke actions are distinct actions with default deny.
- Ontology rule: retail-order maps to an ontology object type with per-tenant subclassing and source-system provenance.
- Observability rule: emit transition metric, trace span, log digest, and immutable audit event for each state change.
- Failure handling: partial acceptance, rail outage, counterparty revocation, jurisdiction hold, and duplicate submission all resolve to explicit states.
- Rollback: settlement rollback means compensating DealSet transition plus audit evidence; destructive row deletion is forbidden.
