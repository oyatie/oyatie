### X-1 — `[CRITICAL]` Accepted ADRs depend on PROPOSED ADRs (dependency-status inversion / unratified foundation)
Nearly every Accepted ADR in scope hard-cites **ADR-0007 (Cedar, `status: proposed`)** and **ADR-0002 (tenant/identity kernel, `status: proposed`)** as the substrate they bind to:

### X-2 — `[CRITICAL]` "Foundry" is canon-dead but lives in 4 Accepted ADRs (contradicts ADR-0335/0363/0512 + canon D-FOUNDRY-CLARIFY)
ADR-0335 (`Accepted` — foundry-microservice-retired-absorbed-by-intelligence) and ADR-0363 (`Accepted` — retire-agentic-vcs-foundry) retire the foundry context; ADR-0512:57 (`Accepted`, founder-locked) states **"The `foundry` name remains eradicated."** Yet the in-scope Accepted ADRs still enshrine it:

### ADR-0001 — Cohesion thesis (one product, flat catalog, six substrates)
**Disposition: AMEND**

**(1) Contradictions**
- `[CRITICAL]` vs ADR-0512 / canon D-PURESPLIT: ADR-0001:34–69 codifies a single flat catalog with "no vertical, arm, product group, or platform grouping in code or architecture" and lists `Foundry (internal-only)` (:65) + `Runtime substrate: cloud-tenancy, cloud-iam …` (:60–63) as flat entries. Canon D-PURESPLIT (canon:172) now mandates the `{oya,cloud}/` two-tree split — i.e. a deliberate product-vs-platform **grouping** at the top level. ADR-0001's "no grouping" invariant is now partially false: there IS a structural oya/cloud boundary (the dogfooding seam, canon D-LAYER:70). Not fatal — the oya/cloud split is org/substrate layering, not sales-segment grouping — but the prose must be reconciled.
- `[CRITICAL]` Foundry (see X-2): :65, :80–82, :87 enshrine retired `oya-foundry-*` substrate owners. Contradicts ADR-0335/0363 (Accepted) + canon D-FOUNDRY-CLARIFY.
- `[HIGH]` vs canon D-LAYER (canon:70) + ADR-0242: ADR-0001:60–63 lists `cloud-*` substrates as flat-catalog peers of products. Canon now frames cloud as the **substrate that hosts products as tenant workloads** (recursion/dogfood). The flat-peer framing loses the layering the canon makes load-bearing.
- `[MED]` Depends on Proposed ADR-0007 (X-1): :81–82.

**(2) Hyperscaler lens**
- `[MED]` Over-broad day-0 surface: the flat catalog (:37–58) enumerates ~25 customer-facing products PLUS ~17 substrate features PLUS ~10 runtime substrates as one undifferentiated catalog. A hyperscaler (AWS/GCP) separates the **platform plane** (IAM, KMS, compute, billing) from the **application plane** (the SaaS products) precisely so the platform can be sold independently and the blast radii differ. The "everything is shared / flat" stance under-engineers the platform/product boundary that canon D-LAYER + D-SCOPE-UNIFY (sell cloud AND products) requires. The cohesion *thesis* (one identity/audit/tenancy) is exemplary and hyperscaler-aligned; the *flat-catalog-with-no-grouping* mechanism is the part that drifted.
- `[GOOD]` The "six shared substrates, no microservice re-implements them" invariant (:28) is exactly the Google-monorepo / one-IAM discipline. KEEP the substrate-singleton idea.

**(3) Patterns / anti-patterns**
- ANTI: forbidden-term leakage as architecture (`Foundry`, `platform`/`cloud-*` flat peers) — :65, :60–63.
- ANTI: CI-enforced invariant (`oya-check-cohesion`, :102) cited as live while ADR-0007/0002 it depends on are Proposed — claim-ceiling risk (canon D-DOCTRINE:181 "robust, not false").
- GOOD: `ForbiddenPattern` enum (:88–99) is a clean machine-checkable cohesion contract — exemplary; KEEP, retarget away from `foundry`.

**(3) Patterns / anti-patterns**

### ADR-0031 — Ads + Analytics microservice (singleton ads-gate)
**Disposition: AMEND**

**(1) Contradictions**
- `[LOW]` Routes to Proposed 0007 Cedar (:84, :37) + 0008 DUBO. X-1.
- `[MED]` vs canon D4 (X-4): :118 `oya-analytics-warehouse-adapter — ClickHouse OLAP tier impl (ADR-0045)`. Vendored OLAP as endpoint.
- `[LOW]` Foundry: none significant (clean). Naming is canon-aligned (`oya-ads-*`, `oya-analytics-*`).

**(2) Hyperscaler lens**
- `[HIGH]` Singleton-gate SPOF: ADR-0031:32 "The gate is a singleton" + :134 "gate outage = no ads served anywhere." A single global serving chokepoint for ALL ad sourcing is a deliberate correctness-over-availability trade. Hyperscalers (Google Ads) achieve the same policy guarantee via a replicated/sharded gate with a consistent policy snapshot, NOT a literal singleton. As written this is a fleet-wide availability SPOF. The privacy intent is right; the "singleton" mechanism is a scale/availability ceiling — should be "logically-single, physically-replicated gate."

**(3) Patterns / anti-patterns**
- ANTI: literal singleton as a fleet-wide serving chokepoint (:32, :134) — reframe to replicated-with-single-policy-source-of-truth.
- GOOD: singleton-SOURCING rule (one consent/policy authority) is the right invariant; separate the *policy authority* (legitimately single) from the *serving path* (must replicate). KEEP the policy-authority half.
